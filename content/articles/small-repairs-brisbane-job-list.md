One clear job list makes a small-repair enquiry easier to assess. List each fault separately, photograph it and say which items matter most. Grouping the requests gives a handyman a better view of the work; it does not guarantee every task will fit into one visit or cost a fixed amount.

River City Handyman's [Brisbane services](/services/) include repair, installation and assembly work. Start with the complete list, even if you only want the most urgent items addressed first. The rest of the list helps identify parts, access or separate assessments that could affect the booking.

## Walk through the home once

Use room names to organise the enquiry. “Kitchen: cupboard beside sink does not close” is clearer than “fix cupboards”. “Bedroom two: handle moves on both sides” is clearer than “door problem”. Keep each description focused on what happens during normal use rather than guessing a repair method.

Photograph the whole item, then the affected area. A close-up gives detail; the wider picture shows size, position and access. You do not need to dismantle anything or test an unsafe fault to prepare the list. Note any earlier repair attempt and keep supplied replacement parts available for checking.

## Separate priorities from convenient extras

Put faults affecting ordinary access or essential use near the top. Identify any immediate safety or security concern clearly and seek the suitable urgent repair route rather than waiting for a routine grouped appointment. Decorative requests can be described separately so they do not obscure the reason for the enquiry.

For ordinary work, explain the deadline and its reason. A bed needed after delivery and a picture you would like hung eventually have different scheduling needs. Ask what can reasonably be covered before that deadline; do not assume adding several little jobs leaves the appointment unchanged.

## Make a simple worksheet

| Location | Request | Information to attach |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen | Cupboard or handle adjustment | Wide and close photos, quantity and supplied parts |
| Bedroom | Internal door catches or will not latch | Door and frame photos plus a symptom description |
| Living room | Shelf or picture installation | Product dimensions, wall photo and intended position |
| Home office | Boxed furniture assembly | Product links, quantities and delivery status |

These are example enquiry categories, not a promise that every item will be accepted. If you are unsure whether a request is within scope, include it as a question. Work involving fixed electrical systems, plumbing, structural defects or building fire safety needs the appropriate qualified assessment rather than being added as an ordinary odd job.

## Name what you already supply

Say whether you have bought handles, shelves, brackets or furniture. Add product links and quantities, and mention incomplete deliveries or missing fittings. A photograph of a packet can help identify what you have, but compatibility still needs checking before it is used.

Ask about parts sourcing, collecting materials, moving furniture and disposing of packaging. Those tasks consume time and should be agreed separately. For boxed items, the [room-by-room furniture assembly checklist](/blog/furniture-assembly-brisbane-room-checklist/) helps identify the product and workspace details that a repair list alone may omit.

## Give the quote a practical scope

Describe the outcome you want. “Handle works securely” and “replace every handle to match” are different requests. “Mount shelf” and “supply shelf, mount it and repair an old fixing area” also involve different work. Photographs do not automatically reveal hidden damage or every required part.

A useful quote discussion covers accepted tasks, materials, preparation, finish, exclusions and whether more than one visit may be needed. There are no invented hourly rates or minimum charges here. Ask for the terms applying to your actual list before deciding whether to proceed.

## Add property access and authorisation

Include your suburb, parking or loading arrangements, stairs and any lift booking. For managed buildings, share approved work hours and the person who can authorise the tasks. Mention occupied rooms or furniture that restricts access rather than leaving those details until arrival.

Tenants and property managers should agree the repair route and scope before arranging non-urgent work. The [RTA repairs information](https://www.rta.qld.gov.au/repairs) helps you find the relevant Queensland guidance. Keep approval records and identify which requests are repairs and which are new fixtures or alterations.
