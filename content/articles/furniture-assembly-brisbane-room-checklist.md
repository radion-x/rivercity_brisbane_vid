Organise a furniture assembly booking by room, with the exact products, quantities and delivery status on one list. That makes it easier to discuss the work, identify anything missing and decide which items need priority.

River City Handyman accepts enquiries for [furniture assembly in Brisbane](/service/flatpack-assembly/). Start with product links and your suburb. Assembly, moving packages, wall anchoring and disposal should be confirmed as separate parts of the agreed scope.

## Start with the room you need first

If you are setting up several rooms, identify the essential item rather than assuming every task can be finished together. A bed needed for the first night and a desk needed for work may matter more than a storage unit that can wait.

| Room | Details to put on your list |
| --- | --- |
| Bedroom | Bed model, storage drawers, bedside units and available floor space |
| Living room | TV unit, shelving, product dimensions and intended positions |
| Home office | Desk model, chair, storage and whether any supplied electrical components are involved |
| Storage area | Unit height, wall position and manufacturer anchoring instructions |

Use this as a planning worksheet, not a promise that every product or alteration fits an ordinary assembly booking. Send unusual items for assessment first.

## Match the delivery to the product list

Confirm which boxes have arrived. Keep labels and instructions so the assembler can match each package to the right item. Explain damage, missing packages or assembly already attempted.

Opening every box at once can make the list harder to manage. If you do unpack, keep small fittings with their own product. Contact the supplier about missing parts and tell the assembler before the visit so you can agree whether the booking should change.

## Check the route into each room

Describe stairs, narrow doorways, lift access and where the packages will be stored. In a raised home, note the actual route and steps to the work area; in an apartment, note the floor, loading arrangements and lift rules. Do not assume the assembler will carry heavy deliveries from the street or another level unless that work is agreed.

Choose the final furniture position before assembly. Mention skirting, existing fittings or doors that may affect the fit. Product dimensions should be compared with the actual room, not just the empty floor area visible in a photo.

## Make space for the assembly

The workspace must allow the product's assembly method as well as access around it. [IKEA's assembly information](https://www.ikea.com/au/en/customer-service/knowledge/articles/56b45bc4-8094-4064-9ae6-e249fb90ca61.html) covers preparation for its service, including space and moving arrangements. Check the instructions for your own product and confirm the workspace with your assembler.

Move fragile items away from the work area and discuss floor protection. If you cannot clear the room, send a wide photo before booking rather than leaving the issue until arrival.

## Separate assembly from anchoring

Read the product's stability and anchoring instructions. The [ACCC explains the risk of toppling furniture](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/publication/toppling-furniture-why-anchor-it) and the role of anchoring. Ask how the required fixing work will be assessed for your wall.

If you rent, check permission requirements before wall drilling. Supply the relevant instructions and written conditions with the job details. A freestanding furniture assembly request should not quietly become a built-in renovation or electrical installation.

Tell us if you need help moving boxes or dealing with packaging. Ask whether these tasks are offered, included or excluded. Keep the order and product information until the item has been checked, and follow the supplier's advice about retaining packaging for a damaged item.
